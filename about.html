Our purpose

Stronger businesses. Stronger community.

Enterprising Taumarunui Incorporated (ETI) is a voluntary community business group that helps local businesses engage in the community, grow and develop, and supports economic development in the Taumarunui area.

Membership is open to businesses, companies, trades, firms and individuals in the Taumarunui area. ETI provides business listings and works proactively on community projects.

Our place · King Country

About Taumarunui

Our place has been loved by generations of families for our naturally beautiful environment, our colourful heritage and our caring community.

A gateway to outdoor adventure

Taumarunui sits on the Adventure Highway (State Highway 4) at the start of the Forgotten World Highway in the central King Country of the North Island. Part of the Ruapehu District, it is centrally located for access to the dual-listed World Heritage Tongariro National Park, and is a gateway to Whanganui National Park and the Pureora Timber Trail.

Along these highways are recreational activities and outdoor pursuits including canoeing, jetboating, fishing, golfing, mountain biking, cycle trails, walks, tramping and rail cart rides. Taumarunui is an ideal base for these activities, with the population increasing during the ski and summer seasons.

A rural service centre

Historically a rural service and shopping centre, Taumarunui serves surrounding sheep, cattle and deer farms, with forestry plantations also a key feature of the region. It increasingly serves visitors exploring Mt Ruapehu, Mt Tongariro and the Tongariro Crossing, the Whanganui River and the Pureora Forest Timber Trail.

Heritage and local life

Our history includes railways, sawmills and coal. Taumarunui is known for farming, railway history, trout fishing, gardens and weaving, as well as its golf course. Today, visitors also enjoy Forgotten World Adventures rail carts, the Timber Trail and adventures on the Whanganui River.

The meeting point of the Whanganui and Ongarue rivers at Cherry Grove is historically important in Māori culture and is a place for water-based activities.

Explore the region

Indicative driving times from the original visitor information are around two hours from Hamilton, three and a half hours from Auckland and 45 minutes to Whakapapa Village. Allow for road and weather conditions.

Pureora Forest Park to the north-east has stands of ancient native trees, abundant bird life, walking tracks and mountain biking opportunities. From picnics and short walks to more energetic adventures, the area's bush, waterfalls, lakes, mountains and rivers offer experiences for a range of ages and abilities.
